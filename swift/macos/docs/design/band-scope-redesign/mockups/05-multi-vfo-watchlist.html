05 · Multi-VFO + watchlist

← gallery · research notes

Variant 01 plus a second VFO cursor for radios with sub-receivers (K3 / IC-7610 / FTDX-101 / FT-DX10), a persistent watch-list strip below the scope, and a "history trail" of where the radio has been in the last few minutes. Forward-looking power-user variant — most users don't have a sub-receiver radio, but the architecture decisions for cursor tracking should leave room for this so we don't repaint the interaction layer in a year.

A Two cursors, two colors. VFO A is red (matches the existing convention), VFO B is purple. Both are drawn as horizontal lines with filter passband shading and dedicated readouts in the header. The mini-map rail shows both VFO positions with A and B labels. B Tooltip offers both tune actions. Hovering a spot, the tooltip footer shows ⏎ tune A · ⌥⏎ tune B. This is the entire reason multi-VFO matters in a contest: you can dump VFO B onto a fresh mult while VFO A is still finishing the QSO on its run frequency. C VFO A history trail. A faint trail of small red dots in the right margin shows where VFO A has been in the last few minutes, with opacity decay. Useful for "what was that frequency I just tuned away from?" without scrubbing through the radio's memory. D Watch list as a strip, not a panel. The watch markers from variant 01 grow into a real horizontal strip below the scope. Each pill shows the frequency, optional note, and age. Color of the left border indicates freshness (green < 5 m, yellow < 15 m, gray older). Click a pill to recenter the scope, double-click to tune VFO B. E m drops a marker, ⌘m drops a marker with a note prompt. The note is freeform — "loud DX call", "VK net 0700z", "FT8 noise" — and shows up in the watch pill. Useful enough that it's worth a modal prompt.
! Why this is forward-looking, not Phase 1. Most users don't have a sub-receiver radio. The reason to mock it up now is that the architecture decisions for cursor tracking — how the scope holds "the cursor's frequency" vs. "the radio's frequency" — should leave room for two cursors from day one. If we paint the variant 01 single-cursor version into the code as a permanent assumption, retrofitting two cursors means a refactor of the gesture handlers, the readout views, the RadioManager shim, and the history trail rendering. Cheap to design for now, expensive to bolt on later.

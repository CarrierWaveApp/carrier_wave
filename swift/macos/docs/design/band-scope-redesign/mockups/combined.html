01 · Single-band, refined

CWSweep · combined view

Same single-band layout as today, with every interaction pain point fixed in place. Lowest-risk shipping path: every change is a localized bug-fix or feature add, none of them require new top-level views. Green markers indicate the additions.

Mult New band slot RBN Worked Watch marker
A Mini-map rail. 38 pt left rail showing the entire band with spot density and mult markers, plus a draggable viewport rectangle for the zoomed region. Click anywhere on the rail to recenter. B Cursor crosshair + tooltip-follows-cursor. A horizontal line tracks the mouse and the spot tooltip pops next to it instead of being pinned 100 pt from the right edge. Header shows the cursor frequency live (14118.4 in the screenshot). C Watch frequency markers. Press m to drop an orange flag at the cursor frequency without tuning. Markers persist until removed and are listed in the bottom bar so you can come back later. D VFO and cursor frequency in dedicated header readouts. No more red label overlapping the axis. Two adjacent boxes — blue for the cursor under the mouse, red for the actual VFO. Both visible at all times. E Sub-band shading at a visible opacity. 12% instead of 4%, with section labels (CW / DIGITAL, SSB / PHONE) and a dashed boundary line at 14070 kHz. You can actually see them now. F Zoom controls + named region presets. Explicit − / + / FIT buttons in the bottom bar plus a named-region picker. CW DX (000–040) jumps directly to the DX window with the right zoom; other presets (RTTY, SSB DX, FT8) are one click away. Keyboard shortcuts in the bottom bar make all of this discoverable.
+ Other invisible fixes baked in: zoom-aware handleTap coordinate math (the wrong-frequency-when-zoomed bug from 00-current), per-band zoom state preservation across autoSwitchBand, mode-aware click-to-tune snap radius using the radio's filter width, click-empty-space-to-tune, scroll-pans-when-zoomed (shift to zoom), and active filter chips inline in the header instead of "N sources hidden."

A One column per band, fully independent. The contest operator can scan all five HF bands at a glance and answer "where are the mults?" without changing band. Heat bars at the bottom of each column show the spot rate over the last few minutes — instantly visible "10 m is dead, 15 m is hot." B Active band has a hard green outline. The 20 m column is the one the radio is tuned to. The VFO red bar in that column shows where in the band you are. The other columns are read-only until you click them. C Mults are visually loud. Bold green for new entities, red for not-yet-worked, orange for new band slots. NEW and BS tags reinforce the color so you don't have to remember which is which. Dupes fade to gray. D Click any spot in any column to QSY both band and frequency in one action. The keyboard model uses arrows for navigation, 1–5 as direct column jumps, and ⏎ to tune the current selection.
! Tradeoffs. You give up the live spectrum and waterfall to gain cross-band situational awareness. Best on wide monitors. On a 1280 pt laptop, four columns are comfortable; five is tight; six requires hiding bands. Likely a per-role preset (Contester role defaults to stacked, Hunter defaults to single).

A Every HF band visible at once. The only layout where the operator can answer "is there anything on 6 m?" or "did 80 m wake up?" without changing band. Each cell is a self-contained summary: spot count, mult count, density histogram, recent activity heat strip, age of last spot. B Drill-down via expansion. ! Tradeoffs. Lowest interaction density per band — you can't do real S&P from the grid view alone. The grid is only useful if it's paired with the expanded view, which means the layout commits 580 + 812 = 1392 pt to itself. On a laptop screen this leaves no room for the QSO log, the entry field, or any other panel. Best as a full-window mode, not a permanent layout.

A Active band scope is essentially unchanged from variant 01. Live spectrum, waterfall, mini-map, cursor crosshair, sub-band shading — all the fixes from 01 apply here. Operators don't have to relearn anything about how the main scope works. B Band rail replaces the existing footer chip strip. One row per band, 200 pt wide total. Each row carries the band label, total / mult / new / band-slot counts, an 8-bar density sparkline, and a colored age dot (green = fresh, yellow = warm, gray = cold). Click any row to QSY both band and frequency in one action. C Active band has a green left border. Same visual treatment as variant 02. Inactive bands are subtle; dead bands (160 / 60 / 30 / 17 / 12 / 10 / 6 m in this snapshot) fade to 40% opacity so the eye lands on the bands actually doing something. D Sparklines are signal, not decoration. Each sparkline shows the spot density across the band (low → high frequency, left → right). Mults are colored green so the eye picks them up without reading numbers. The shape of the sparkline tells you whether the band is bottom-heavy CW activity, top-heavy SSB activity, or evenly spread. E Keyboard model. ⌘1–⌘0 for direct band jumps (matches the muscle memory of every contest logger ever), ⇥ to cycle to the next band with new mults — that's the "I'm running out of fresh stations on 20 m, what's hot?" shortcut.
! Why this is recommended. Smallest delta from today's layout. Active scope is the variant 01 fix-set (which we wanted anyway). The rail replaces an existing UI element rather than adding a new one. The 200 pt rail eats only 14 % of the horizontal space and gives you situational awareness for all 11 HF bands. The next step up (stacked, variant 02) gives you more density per band but costs the live spectrum, which we don't want to give up by default.

05 · Multi-VFO + watchlist

CWSweep · combined view

Variant 01 plus a second VFO cursor for radios with sub-receivers (K3 / IC-7610 / FTDX-101 / FT-DX10), a persistent watch-list strip below the scope, and a "history trail" of where the radio has been in the last few minutes. Forward-looking power-user variant — most users don't have a sub-receiver radio, but the architecture decisions for cursor tracking should leave room for this so we don't repaint the interaction layer in a year.

A Two cursors, two colors. VFO A is red (matches the existing convention), VFO B is purple. Both are drawn as horizontal lines with filter passband shading and dedicated readouts in the header. The mini-map rail shows both VFO positions with A and B labels. B Tooltip offers both tune actions. Hovering a spot, the tooltip footer shows ⏎ tune A · ⌥⏎ tune B. This is the entire reason multi-VFO matters in a contest: you can dump VFO B onto a fresh mult while VFO A is still finishing the QSO on its run frequency. C VFO A history trail. A faint trail of small red dots in the right margin shows where VFO A has been in the last few minutes, with opacity decay. Useful for "what was that frequency I just tuned away from?" without scrubbing through the radio's memory. D Watch list as a strip, not a panel. The watch markers from variant 01 grow into a real horizontal strip below the scope. Each pill shows the frequency, optional note, and age. Color of the left border indicates freshness (green < 5 m, yellow < 15 m, gray older). Click a pill to recenter the scope, double-click to tune VFO B. E m drops a marker, ⌘m drops a marker with a note prompt. The note is freeform — "loud DX call", "VK net 0700z", "FT8 noise" — and shows up in the watch pill. Useful enough that it's worth a modal prompt.
! Why this is forward-looking, not Phase 1. Most users don't have a sub-receiver radio. The reason to mock it up now is that the architecture decisions for cursor tracking — how the scope holds "the cursor's frequency" vs. "the radio's frequency" — should leave room for two cursors from day one. If we paint the variant 01 single-cursor version into the code as a permanent assumption, retrofitting two cursors means a refactor of the gesture handlers, the readout views, the RadioManager shim, and the history trail rendering. Cheap to design for now, expensive to bolt on later.
